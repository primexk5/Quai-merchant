# TripplePay || Marchants — Pitch Deck Script (v2)

Creative direction: **"The internet moved. Money didn't. We fixed that."**

The whole deck tells one story: money is the slowest thing on the internet, the fast lane already exists (Quai), and we are the storefront that connects them. Every slide is one idea, one feeling, one ask.

Length: ~7 minutes, 13 slides. Cut slides 9 or 11 if you need to go shorter.

---

## Slide 1 — Cover

**On-screen headline:** Payments should just work.

**On-screen copy:**
- TripplePay || Marchants
- The checkout layer for Quai
- Fast. Low-cost. Verifiable.

**Visual:** The checkout mock — Premium Hoodie, ₦5,000, a big "Pay with Quai" button, and a green checkmark.

**What you say (20s):**
"Payments should just work. That's the whole company. TripplePay || Marchants is a checkout layer for the Quai blockchain — it lets any merchant accept crypto payments with a button their customers already know how to use. My name is [Name], and I'm the [role]."

## Slide 2 — Hook: A day in the life

**On-screen headline:** Ada sold a hoodie at 9:00 AM. She'll see the money tomorrow.

**On-screen copy:**
- Sale at 9:00 AM → money at 9:00 AM *tomorrow*
- In that time, the internet sold the same hoodie 100,000 times over

**Visual:** A timeline — one line for Ada's money (stops at "TOMORROW"), one line for the internet (a straight fast line). The contrast should be obvious at a glance.

**What you say (40s):**
"Meet Ada. She runs a clothing store in Lagos. At 9:00 AM she sells a hoodie — customer pays, everyone's happy. But Ada won't see that money until tomorrow. Maybe later, depending on the weekend. Meanwhile, the internet did its thing: it moved, it clicked, it settled. Ada's money is the slowest thing on the internet. The question of this deck is simple — why?"

---

## Slide 3 — Problem

**On-screen headline:** Money is the slowest thing on the internet.

**On-screen copy (three cards, no paragraphs):**
- **Tomorrow:** T+1 settlement on standard rails — the money moves after the sale
- **1.5–2% + fees:** every sale pays a toll
- **Worse abroad:** cross-border adds card restrictions and FX on top

**Visual:** Three big numbers with one-word labels. Nothing else.

**What you say (30s):**
"Digital commerce runs at internet speed. Settlement runs at bank speed. Standard rails pay out next working day, take 1.5 to 2 percent, and cross-border just stacks more fees and rules on top. The customer experience got magical — the money experience stayed stuck in the 1980s."

**On-screen copy:**
- One line of code: `<PayWithQuai amount="18500" currency="NGN" orderId="ord_1024" />`

**What you say (30s):**

## Slide 6 — The 10-second loop

**On-screen headline:** Pay → Verified → Paid. Ten seconds, end to end.

**On-screen copy (the 5-step flow):**
1. Merchant adds the widget
2. Customer connects wallet + pays
3. Quai verifies the order
4. Relayer sends the webhook
5. Merchant marks it PAID

**Visual:** A circular loop with the five steps, plus the proof point: "checkout → on-chain verification → webhook → fulfillment."

**What you say (30s):**
"The loop is complete and short: the customer pays, Quai verifies it in the open, our relayer confirms it, the merchant's system marks the order paid. It's the same feeling as clicking 'Pay' on any website — the difference is the truth: the payment is verified on-chain, in seconds, for less than a cent."

## Slide 10 — Moat

**On-screen headline:** Anyone can copy a button. Nobody can copy a network.

**On-screen copy (four moats):**
- **Integration** — SDK + plugins + docs make us part of a merchant's stack
- **Reliability** — monitoring, retries, reconciliation build trust
- **Ecosystem** — more TripplePay merchants → more reason for wallets to support the rail
- **Data** — merchant analytics that improve product decisions

**Visual:** The button on the left (small, easy). The network on the right (big, tangled, valuable).

**What you say (30s):**
"The moat is not the button — anyone can build a button in a weekend. The moat is the network: the integrations merchants rely on, the reliability they trust, the ecosystem effects — more merchants attract more wallets — and the data that compounds. Each one makes leaving harder and staying better."
